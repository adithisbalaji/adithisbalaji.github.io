About Me

In the summer of ‘24, I helped rebuild a deteriorating, water-damaged home for an indigent family in Appalachia, Virginia. Their cabinets, filled with carb-heavy and processed foods demarcated the link between affordability and access to fresh produce. Prompted by this setting, I began studying the health implications of food deserts while interrogating the multifaceted question of how economic and political conditions influence health outcomes.

Consequently, I began working with Dr. Susan Kansagra, then North Carolina State Health Director, through the NC Department of Health and Human Services during the state's 2023 Medicaid expansion. My outreach efforts primarily targeted rural populations, particularly individuals who faced language barriers, limited internet access, or were unaware of the expanded Medicaid eligibility criteria and their implications for healthcare coverage. I collaborated with state senators and health officials to research and disseminate updated eligibility criteria through simple-language informational flyers and community-based information sessions. Through these efforts, I established partnerships with seven health advocacy organizations and nine faith-based communities to extend outreach to residents who might otherwise have remained unaware of the policy changes. Following the expansion and our outreach efforts, North Carolina's Medicaid enrollment grew from approximately 2.9 million to 3.2 million beneficiaries.

To further examine health policy, I researched disparities in the geographic distribution of healthcare professionals across rural and urban counties at the North Carolina General Assembly, examining the financial considerations that influence providers' decisions to practice and remain in rural communities. Comparing workforce shortages across multiple health professions, I investigated how educational debt and the prospect of more manageable loan repayment in urban areas can influence career placement, contributing to persistent shortages in rural North Carolina. Under legislative guidance, I helped develop two Senate bills, SB 181 and SB 277, proposing strategies to address these disparities through medical school tuition subsidies, loan forgiveness programs, and financial incentives that reward providers for sustained service in rural communities.

Currently, I attend the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, pursuing a B.S.P.H. in Health Policy and Management through the Gillings School of Global Public Health and a B.S. in Biochemistry. Through these complementary disciplines, I intend to understand how molecular interactions dictate the pathology that allows for rapid viral mutation. In turn, this immunological foundation will inform my approach to preventive care, and resultingly, the large-scale intervention strategies commonly evaluated during the development of health policy. I hope to apply this intensive, interdisciplinary education to infectious disease vaccine research and, ultimately, evaluate their broader implications for U.S. health legislation.
