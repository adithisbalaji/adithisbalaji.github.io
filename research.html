Research Statement

As Nobel laureate and virologist Joshua Lederberg emphasizes, understanding viral evolution is fundamental to addressing the persistent challenges viruses pose to human health and the development of effective vaccines. I have pursued this interest through my research on the molecular mechanisms of viral antigenicity at the Duke Human Vaccine Institute. At Duke, I conduct computational analyses of over 400 HIV-1 Envelope (Env) structures, using Python-based methods to quantify molecular geometry and principal component analysis to characterize mutation-associated structural variation. My current research investigates which structural features of the HIV-1 Envelope glycoprotein, if any, determine antibody epitope accessibility and binding affinity across experimentally-induced mutations. I hope to leverage this immunological foundation to inform large-scale preventive care strategies and ultimately examine how advances in infectious disease research can shape U.S. public health legislation.
